# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two distinct audiences, one product:

- **Parents / guardians** (primary buyer). Often anxious, busy, not technical. They open the app to answer one question fast: "Is my child OK online right now?" Context: glancing on a phone between other tasks. They need calm authority and clarity, not a wall of data.
- **Children / teens** (the monitored device). They did not choose this app. Trust is fragile. They open it to understand what is and isn't seen, and to feel the product is honest and on their side, not spying. Context: a kid's Android phone, often skeptical.

## Product Purpose

Lighthouse is an on-device child-safety monitor. AI runs entirely on the child's phone, reads what's on screen and in messages to flag categories of risk, and sends the parent signals and alerts, never raw content. Success = a parent feels reassured and in control, and a child feels respected and informed rather than surveilled. The privacy line the whole product defends: content is read on-device to check for risk, but never saved, never sent, and the parent never sees the actual words, images, or searches.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, calm, honest. Three words: **reassuring, clear, candid.** Voice is plain and warm, never clinical or alarmist, never cute to the point of trivializing safety. On the child side it is gentle and straight-talking ("here's exactly what I can and can't see"). On the parent side it is composed and authoritative ("we've got this, here's what matters this week"). Emotional goal: lower the user's blood pressure.

## Anti-references

- Surveillance/spyware aesthetics: dark dashboards, red everything, "tracking" connotations, dense telemetry.
- Generic SaaS template look: identical icon+heading+text card grids, hero-metric-with-gradient cliché, dashed-border "add" tiles.
- Over-cute kid apps that undercut the seriousness of safety.
- Fear-driven parental-control marketing (cracked-phone imagery, alarm-red urgency).

## Design Principles

- **Calm is the feature.** Reduce visual noise so the one thing that matters stands out. Whitespace and hierarchy over density.
- **Practice the honesty we promise.** The UI should make the read-on-device / never-shared line legible and obvious, mirroring the product's privacy stance. Transparency screens are first-class, not fine print.
- **Two voices, one system.** Child surfaces lean reassuring and friendly; parent surfaces lean composed and authoritative. Same tokens, different warmth.
- **Depth, not decoration.** Premium feel comes from soft elevation, precise spacing rhythm, and restrained color, not gradients-for-their-own-sake or ornament.
- **Earn the cyan.** The brand blue is a scarce accent for what's live/actionable, not a flood.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text >= 13px with sufficient contrast on tinted backgrounds; never convey state by color alone (pair the cyan/severe colors with icon + label). Respect reduced-motion (entrance/press animations degrade to opacity only). Tap targets >= 44px. Content read by children must stay plain-language and literal.
